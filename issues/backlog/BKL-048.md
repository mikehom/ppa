## Summary

Boolean guards [not is-nil(x)] act as identity, Case 1 maps nil-deref in [x:=y.sel] to {kill_x(...)} without raising a fault, and absent GC inflates 𝒫(SG) past 50 graphs on 7-block list reversal.

## Technical Analysis / Mechanism

Three operational pitfalls limit naive `§2.6` implementations: (1) Boolean tests such as `[not is-nil(x)]` act as identity transfers, failing to prune null branches; (2) `[x := y.sel]` Case 1 silently maps null/missing-selector dereferences to `{kill_x(...)}` rather than aborting or emitting a fault, allowing post-crash paths to survive; (3) Neither `kill_x` nor `kill_{x.sel}` removes unreachable nodes from `n_∅`, pushing simple 7-block list reversal (`Exercise* 2.21`) above 50 abstract shape graphs unless GC, allocation-site naming (`n_{ℓ,X}`), or single-summary merging is introduced.

Guardrail: Emit client null-deref diagnostics from Shape∘(ℓ) before Case 1 transfer, pair with [b]^{ℓ₁,ℓ₂} branch splits, and adopt GC / n_{ℓ,X} / single-summary merging [148, 149]. (Refs: G-U2b-17, G-U2b-23, G-U2b-24, G-U2b-28).
