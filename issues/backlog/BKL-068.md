## Summary

Example 4.15 special-cases only int_1 = int_2 = bot; for int_1 = bot and int_2 = [z_3, z_4], inf(bot) = inf > z_3 and sup(bot) = -inf < z_4 trigger LB_K(inf, z_3) and UB_K(-inf, z_4) (giving [-inf, inf] when K = empty).

## Technical Analysis / Mechanism

On p. 229, Example 4.15's piecewise int_1 nabla_K int_2 formula filters out only the double-bottom pair int_1 = int_2 = bot before branching to [LB_K(inf(int_1), inf(int_2)), UB_K(sup(int_1), sup(int_2))]. Because Example 4.10 defines inf(bot) = inf and sup(bot) = -inf, step 1 with int_1 = bot and int_2 = [z_3, z_4] satisfies inf(bot) > z_3 and sup(bot) < z_4, immediately jumping to [-inf, inf] when K = empty.

Guardrail: Enforce bot nabla_K int_2 = int_2 and int_1 nabla_K bot = int_1 short-circuits in all interval widenings (cf. Exercise 4.6(4)) (Refs: F-U4a-05, G-U4a-13).
