## Summary

§2.5 embellishes a monotone framework, yet the book itself flags an important snag: the call and return transfer functions for large assumption sets are in general not monotone.

## Technical Analysis / Mechanism

Adding a fact at a call site replaces the exact context key instead of extending it, so f(Z1) ⊑ f(Z2) can fail for Z1 ⊑ Z2 (derived from the definitions on p. 100). Termination of the worklist algorithm is not lost: the termination part of the proof of Lemma 2.29 uses only that each update joins into a strictly larger value and that the lattice satisfies the Ascending Chain Condition [PPA Lemma 2.29, p. 75] (book p. 75; PDF p. 94), [PPA §2.4, p. 76] (book p. 76; PDF p. 95). What is lost is the guarantee that the result is the least (MFP) solution: the correctness part of the proof uses the monotonicity of the transfer functions [PPA §2.4, p. 77] (book p. 77; PDF p. 96).

Guardrail: L2: treat the snag as a precision and least-solution issue, not a non-termination issue, and pick one of the book's remedies: (1) solving techniques for weakly monotonic systems of equations, referenced in the Concluding Remarks [PPA Chapter 2 Concluding Remarks, p. 129] (book p. 129; PDF p. 148); (2) the more approximate, monotone definition that relaxes the equality on the context component to an inclusion; (3) small assumption sets [PPA §2.5, p. 100] (book p. 100; PDF p. 119). Never feed exact large-assumption-set context keys into a monotone solver without stating the snag.
