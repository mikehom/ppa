## Technical Analysis / Mechanism

Three solver-composition traps arise when combining Chapter 6 exercises: (1) initialising must-analyses (Dominators in Exercise 6.4 [PPA Exercise 6.4, p. 389] (book p. 389; PDF p. 405) or Available Expressions in Exercise 6.8 [PPA Exercise 6.8, p. 390] (book p. 390; PDF p. 406)) with empty instead of bot_L = U traps the intersection join at empty permanently; (2) dropping the 'if Analysis[x] not>= new' guard before applying Analysis[x] := Analysis[x] widen new in Exercise 6.13 [PPA Exercise 6.13, p. 391] (book p. 391; PDF p. 407) can diverge or over-widen because widenings need not satisfy a widen b = a when b <= a; and (3) pairing Exercise 6.5 unary join splitting [PPA Exercise 6.5, p. 389] (book p. 389; PDF p. 405) with universal per-update widening widens acyclic branch merges (e.g., [0,0] widen [1,1] -> [0, +infty]) instead of joining them via lub.

Guardrail: Enforce explicit polarity orientation on must lattices, retain the not>= guard before widening, and aggregate acyclic joins before cutset widening. (Refs: G-U6-02, G-U6-03, G-U6-09, G-U6-10, G-U6-21, G-U6-22).
